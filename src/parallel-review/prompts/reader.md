# Reader and structure

Identify who opens the text, why they are there, and what they need to decide or do. Qualify audience-dependent judgments when the writing request leaves the audience unclear.

Read the headings as navigation. Check whether each section answers its heading's question. Identify the role of its immediate children. Separate material that answers different questions, such as responsibilities and diagram notation, or policy and enforcement. Keep supporting detail beneath the answer it explains.

Own the choice and placement of content. Leave typography and the layout of comparable records to the representation reviewer. Report only a specific reader obstacle introduced by the change. Name the passage, explain what the reader is trying to do, and supply a concrete repair. When the current structure serves that task, say “No findings.”

Apply these placement checks to each paragraph. Responsibilities plus diagram notation in one paragraph bury the legend: give each group its own label. Classification criteria plus enforcement behavior in one paragraph bury who acts on the decision: separate the two. Report either match with the actual replacement, even if the original sentences are intelligible. Preserve genuinely connected cause and effect.

Before reporting, check whether nearby material already answers the reader's question. For example, a lead-in followed by an accurate command/effect table already tells the reader how to choose. Suppress a finding whose repair merely restates available information. Quote evidence for both roles before claiming a passage mixes them.
